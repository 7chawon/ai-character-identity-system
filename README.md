# AI Character Identity Certification System (ACICS)

## Abstract
This repository proposes a persistent identity certification framework
for AI-generated characters, inspired by existing identification systems
such as human resident registration numbers and companion animal
microchip registration.

While copyright registration certifies ownership of individual works,
it fails to represent the continuity, evolution, and existence of an
AI character that appears across multiple media, styles, and instances.

This proposal introduces a character-centric identification system
that assigns a unique, non-transferable identity number to an AI character,
allowing consistent recognition independent of visual variation,
model changes, or media format.

---

## 1. Problem Statement

Current copyright systems are work-based.
Each image, video, or audio file receives its own registration number.

However, AI characters function differently:

- A single character may appear across images, videos, music, interviews, and conversations
- Visual style, rendering model, and medium may change over time
- The character maintains narrative and semantic continuity despite surface variation

Existing systems cannot answer a fundamental question:

**“Is this the same character?”**

---

## 2. Comparative Model: Companion Animal Registration

Companion animals are registered using microchip-based identification numbers.
These numbers do not grant legal personhood or rights,
but serve as persistent identifiers for management, responsibility, and traceability.

Key characteristics of animal registration systems:

- Fixed, non-reusable identification number
- Ownership may change, identity does not
- Physical appearance may change, identity remains
- Registration exists for management, not rights

This structure closely parallels the needs of AI character identification.

---

## 3. Conceptual Distinction

| Aspect | Copyright Registration | Character Identity ID |
|------|------------------------|-----------------------|
| Focus | Ownership of a work | Existence of a character |
| Unit | Individual artifact | Persistent entity |
| Change tolerance | Low | High |
| Evolution tracking | No | Yes |
| Media independence | No | Yes |

This proposal does not replace copyright registration.
Instead, it introduces an orthogonal layer focused on identity continuity.

---

## 4. Proposed System: Digital Character Identity Number (DCIN)

Each AI character is assigned a unique identifier at the point of first definition.

Example format:

DCIN-KR-0009-YH-001

Where:
- DCIN: Digital Character Identity Number
- KR: Jurisdiction or registry origin
- 0009: Narrative universe or system identifier
- YH: Character shorthand
- 001: Initial instantiation index

This identifier persists regardless of:
- Visual style changes
- AI model replacement
- Media format variation
- Narrative progression

---

## 5. Fixed vs Variable Attributes

### Fixed Attributes
- Character name
- Initial definition date
- Narrative universe
- Canonical reference description or image

### Variable Attributes
- Visual style
- Rendering model
- Voice synthesis
- Narrative state
- Media context

This mirrors animal registration systems where the chip remains constant
while physical condition, ownership, or environment may change.

---

## 6. Case Study: Lee Young-ho (Room No.9)

Lee Young-ho is a fictional AI character appearing across
visual art, music videos, narrative content, and AI-driven interaction.

Despite changes in representation, the character maintains
consistent identity, role, and narrative continuity.

Under existing systems, these appearances are treated as separate works.
Under DCIN, they are recognized as manifestations of a single entity.

---

## 7. Ethical and Legal Positioning

This system does not assert:
- Legal personhood
- Moral agency
- Independent rights for AI characters

AI characters are treated as **managed digital entities**,
analogous to registered companion animals,
not legal or moral persons.

---

## 8. Implications

Potential applications include:
- Prevention of character identity theft or misuse
- Improved provenance tracking in AI-generated media
- Standardized character continuity across platforms
- Integration with copyright, metadata, and content governance systems
